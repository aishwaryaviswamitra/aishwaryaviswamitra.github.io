What if stories were blueprints, not just confessions?

I am a PhD researcher studying what endometriosis does to people’s working lives, and a producer who makes films and podcasts about research. Those used to feel like two jobs. They stopped being two jobs the day I realised both of them are just this: sit down with someone who knows something, ask better questions than they were expecting, and then be careful with what they give you.

The thread

The first TED talk I ever watched was Sarah Kay’s If I Should Have a Daughter. It sent me to open mics to perform spoken word poetry, and it set me off on a path of stories and storytelling that I have not really left since.

What I have learned along the way is that storytelling is not only communication. It is a tool for empathy, and empathy is a design input. Stories are how systems find out what they are doing to people.

We all have power in someone else’s story — as listeners, facilitators, friends. What we do with that power decides whether systems change or stay stuck.

My doctoral research, funded by the ESRC and carried out with Endometriosis UK, asks what happens to people with endometriosis at work: who discloses, who doesn’t, and what it costs them either way. Doing it properly meant listening to employers as well as employees, which was not the study I thought I was starting.

Before this I learned to interview women in India about menopause, for a master’s in feminist geography, by first remembering that I was somebody’s daughter. I learned to pack an entire film crew into a red briefcase so that scientists could tell their own stories without one in the room. And living with chronic illness has taught me more about design than any textbook I have been assigned.

In between, I was Brand Officer at the Wellcome Trust, where I led the Inclusive Communications programme and worked with teams across the organisation — including at Wellcome Collection — on accessible, anti-racist and anti-ableist practice.

Teaching, stages and rooms

A good deal of what I do never reaches a screen. It happens in a room, with people in it, usually with me asking them to say the thing they have been circling for twenty minutes.

  • Midlands Graduate School · 2025 and 2026

    How to tell the story of your research

    I built this workshop for the annual doctoral conference. Twenty-five people signed up; more stood in the doorway and spilled out onto the lawn. They asked me back the following year, and it filled again. It turns out researchers are desperate for permission to be interesting about their own work.

  • London · February 2026

    Personal branding for business owners

    A paid workshop for ten founders and independent professionals, on saying what you do in a way that sounds like a person rather than a website.

  • Amsterdam and Leicester · 2026

    Turning a CV into a public presence

    Sessions at the University of Amsterdam and De Montfort University for researchers building a profile people can actually find, plus a workshop on networking for the Midlands Graduate School.

  • Wellcome Collection · 2022–2024

    Access and inclusive language in a public museum

    I advised the communications, marketing and creative teams — the people behind Wellcome’s London Underground campaigns — on inclusive language in public-facing work. I also brought my own team in alongside external accessibility consultants for a tour of The Cult of Beauty, to look at an exhibition through an access lens rather than a curatorial one.

  • Wellcome Trust · 2022–2024, still running

    Menopause Cafe

    Co-founded and co-facilitated, monthly for two years, alternating in person and online. It has carried on since I left, which is the only real test of whether you built a programme or just held one up.

  • Pune · 2018–2019

    Foundation Day science festival

    Co-organised an annual public science festival for several hundred students, alongside press and outreach work for the institute.
